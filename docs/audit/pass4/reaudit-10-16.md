### The §7.5.4 conflict check (asked specifically)

Result: **the six statements hold, but two clauses around them undercut them.**

- **No neighbour contradicts the order.** `deny > ask > allow` is stated identically in doc 11 R1 (`:178`), §7.5.1 composition (`:1154`), §7.6.1 (`:1228`), and doc 05 `:726`, `:738`, `:1651`.   1. §7.6.3 `:1258-1266` ("managed may unblock") — see the entry below.
  2. §7.5.4(1) `:1213` ("append last" + read-order override) — see the entry below.
  3. `docs/16-operations.md:668` R-ops-79 ("including values the user layer would deny") — the most explicit re-introduction of the upstream defect, and it lives in the ops document, not the authority document.
- `docs/28-implementation-status.md:13,58` also contradicts the order (`deny > allow > ask`; `deny > lease > allow > ask`) — see the `docs/28` section at the end.

- `docs/11-authority-safety.md:1258-1266` — CONTRADICTION (this is the allow-outranks-deny defect in disguise) — §7.6.3's "one widening exception" says a signed managed policy "may unblock" and grants e.g. "egress to `pypi.internal.example` allowed without prompting" when `net.fetch` exists. Under strictest-wins (R1, R5, R10, §7.5.1(2), §7.5.4(2)) a managed `allow` can never outrank a user-level `ask`, so the exception is unimplementable as written; making it work is exactly the defect of letting `allow` beat `ask`/`deny`. — Fix: restate it as a **narrowing** mechanism — a signed managed statement may only *remove an ask requirement it itself created* or *suppress its own layer's ask* for a named destination, recorded in the effective digest and `hzcode doctor`, and it MUST be capped at `ask` by any user `deny`. Add: "no managed grant is ever an `allow` above another layer's `ask` or `deny`".
- `docs/11-authority-safety.md:1213` — CONTRADICTION — §7.5.4(1) "ordinary settings read forward; **policy documents read reversed** so user-global beats repository; organization-managed statements append last" describes an *order-dependent override*, while C1 (`:192`) and §7.6.1 (`:1237`) make the merge an order-independent lattice meet ("never an overwrite").
